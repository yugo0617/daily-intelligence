## Key Stories This Week

### U.S. Labor Market Defies Expectations

In October, the U.S. economy added 165,000 jobs, significantly outpacing the anticipated 65,000. This robust hiring spree led to a marginal decline in the unemployment rate to 4.1%. Professional and business services spearheaded this growth, contributing 70,000 positions. The Federal Reserve, in its recent meeting, opted to maintain interest rates, balancing persistent inflation concerns against a strengthening labor market. Following the employment report, the yield on the two-year Treasury note ascended to 4.51%, indicating market expectations of potential monetary tightening. ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/06/october-2026-jobs-report-unemployment-165000?utm_source=openai))

**Outlook**: The labor market's resilience suggests sustained economic expansion. However, the Federal Reserve's forthcoming decisions will hinge on inflation trajectories and broader economic indicators.

### AI Sector Faces Market Turbulence

The artificial intelligence sector experienced a significant market correction, with the Nasdaq Composite plummeting by up to 5.3% on November 5. This downturn was catalyzed by Intel's earnings report, which hinted at a deceleration in chip demand, challenging the prevailing narrative of AI's immunity to cyclical downturns. Additionally, local legislative measures restricting data-center developments have introduced further uncertainties, potentially delaying critical infrastructure projects. Prominent AI firms, including Nvidia and Micron, have seen their stock values decline by 25% and 55% from their peaks, respectively. ([lemonde.fr](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/11/06/on-wall-street-democrats-midterm-victory-bursts-the-ai-bubble_6773164_19.html?utm_source=openai))

**Outlook**: The AI industry's trajectory remains uncertain, contingent upon regulatory developments and the sector's ability to demonstrate sustainable growth amidst evolving market dynamics.

### Eurozone's Mixed Economic Signals

The International Monetary Fund's latest projections paint a nuanced picture of the eurozone. While the overall growth forecast has been revised upward, France's economy is projected to expand by a mere 0.4% in 2026, a decline from 0.9% in 2025. This slowdown is attributed to factors such as heatwaves, rising unemployment, escalating energy costs, and substantial public deficits. Political uncertainties further exacerbate the economic challenges, with potential implications for investor confidence and fiscal stability. ([lemonde.fr](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/10/13/imf-raises-eurozone-growth-forecast-highlights-sharp-decline-in-french-economy_6758559_19.html?utm_source=openai))

**Outlook**: France's economic trajectory necessitates decisive policy interventions to address structural weaknesses and restore growth momentum.
